What the contract actually says

  • The guarantee is 20 qualified meetings (booked, attended, ICP-fit with BANT) or better ROI than trailing ad spend, “within the 16-week term”. It is not written as “20 MQLs”. “Verified MQL” is defined only as a lead meeting documented criteria and verified in the tracking stack.
  • The refund applies only if DBSync “did not commit a material breach of Section 4 duties”, confirmed by CRM/tracking data, with a 5-business-day cure period after written notice.
  • DBSync's Section 4 duties include: contact new leads within 10 minutes in business hours; call each lead at least 12 times; record 100% of contact attempts in the Aletto CRM; call booked meetings twice daily to confirm; daily CRM log-ins by the sales owner; keep $100–160/day ad budget.

Follow-up speed: the evidence

  • Every conversation thread for all 63 leads was pulled (1,076 events). Only 1 lead received a manual outbound message, and it was an appointment confirmation SMS sent 15 h 57 m after the booking (Matthew D., Sep 23).
  • 0 call records exist. The only “call” evidence is an internal note on Biscuitville's lead, 2 days 7 hours after opt-in: “TEST: CALLED ON TUESDAY AT 09:00 AM”.
  • Against the 10-minute rule, the measured result is: no lead has a logged human contact inside 10 minutes, or inside 2 hours. (The “fastest was 2 hours” figure couldn't be reproduced from the CRM; it may come from DBSync's own tools.)
  • Rajeev's Oct 23 email says “our team is chasing each and every lead.” If true, that activity was not recorded in the Aletto CRM as Section 4 requires.
